Sam Rivera: Aisha, thanks for joining. You're a Program Lead at Brightseed, a small nonprofit. How do you share dashboards with people outside your organization today?
Aisha Rahman: Mostly with funders and our board, and honestly it's held together with tape. I export charts to PDF and email them, or I paste screenshots into our quarterly grant report. When a funder wants an update between reports, I scramble to rebuild the numbers. It's a lot of manual work for a team of, well, mostly me.
Sam Rivera: So the core need is sharing impact data with funders and board members outside Brightseed.
Aisha Rahman: Exactly. Our funders want to see the impact — households served, program outcomes — and they want it to feel current, not a six-month-old snapshot. A live link I could send would be transformative. But it has to be simple. I'm not an IT person, and we don't have one.
Sam Rivera: Simplicity is the theme I'm hearing. What makes a tool too complicated for you?
Aisha Rahman: If setting up a share involves configuring servers, or identity providers, or anything with the word "federation" in it, I'm out. I need: click share, get a link, send it, done. If there's a scary settings page I'll avoid the feature entirely and go back to PDFs.
Sam Rivera: Understood. That said, is there anything sensitive in your dashboards?
Aisha Rahman: Yes, and this worries me. Some of our data is about vulnerable people we serve — names, sometimes contact details, situations. I would never want a funder to see individual personal information. They need aggregates: how many people, what outcomes, trends. So the ability to share only the summary view, with no personal data in it, is really important. A leak of client data would be devastating for the people who trust us.
Sam Rivera: So PII protection matters even at a small scale — maybe more, because trust is everything.
Aisha Rahman: Completely. We can't afford a privacy incident, reputationally or ethically. If sharing makes it easy to accidentally expose a person's details, I won't use it. I'd want the tool to warn me clearly if a share would include personal data, and let me share just the aggregated dashboard.
Sam Rivera: Let's talk cost, since budget is real for nonprofits.
Aisha Rahman: It's the first question my director asks. If external sharing is locked behind an expensive enterprise tier, we can't touch it. Ideally it's part of the base plan, or there's a nonprofit discount. We're used to being priced out of the good features, so anything that treats sharing as standard rather than premium wins my heart.
Sam Rivera: Do you need fancy access controls — passwords, expiry, roles?
Aisha Rahman: A little, and in plain language. Viewer-only is perfect — funders should never edit anything, and I wouldn't want to think about roles beyond "they can look." An expiry date would actually help me, because grant relationships have seasons; a link that quietly expires after the reporting period means I don't have to remember to clean up. And a simple "turn off this link" button in case I send it to the wrong email. But I don't want ten toggles. Sensible defaults, please.
Sam Rivera: So viewer-only default, easy expiry, one-click revoke, minimal choices.
Aisha Rahman: Yes. Decide the safe thing for me and let me override only if I really need to.
Sam Rivera: How do funders and board members actually open these? Devices?
Aisha Rahman: All over the place. Board members are often older and reading on their phones or tablets. So it has to look good on mobile and not require them to install anything or make an account. The second a funder has to "create a password" or "sign up," I lose them, and I get the "it doesn't work" email.
Sam Rivera: No-account viewing is important then, even given the privacy concerns.
Aisha Rahman: It's a balance, I know. For non-sensitive aggregate dashboards, a clean link they just open is ideal. If a particular board doc were sensitive, a simple passcode I can share separately would be a reasonable middle ground — as long as it's not complicated.
Sam Rivera: Last question. What would make this a clear win for Brightseed?
Aisha Rahman: If I could send funders a live, mobile-friendly, aggregate-only link in two clicks, at a price we can afford, and trust that no client's personal data could slip through — that would save me days each quarter and let me focus on the actual programs. That's the dream.
Sam Rivera: That's a wonderful note to end on. Simple, safe, affordable, mobile. Thank you, Aisha.
Aisha Rahman: Thank you for asking the small orgs. We usually get forgotten.
